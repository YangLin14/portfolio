Triton Droids is a UC San Diego student team building a humanoid robot from the ground up, hardware and software in parallel. I joined as an ML Team Member, working with the simulation and reinforcement learning sub-team on the control policies.

Building From the Ground Up

The team splits roughly into two halves: a hardware team handling the mechanical design and electrical systems, and an ML team (simulation + reinforcement learning) writing the policies that control the robot. Because both halves are moving at once, the policies I write have to target a robot whose exact mechanical properties are still being finalized. There is no stable, fully-built platform to assume.

URKL T800

The club entered URKL (Ultimate Robot Knock-out Legend), EngineAI's humanoid combat league where every team uses the T800. The team passed the initial challenge round, and the project stopped before the physical stage in China.

I built the training infrastructure for chaining combat skills inside a single mjlab tracking policy: a MultiMotionLoader and MultiMotionCommand that chain named reference clips (kick, dodge, punch) in one policy, with a closest-entry transition mode that picks the best entry frame in the next clip. I also built the BVH-to-training data path and wrote GPU training runbooks for Colab A100 and the UCSD SDSC cluster. No trained policy exists yet; this is training infrastructure.

Current Projects

  • Falling policy in MuJoCo: in progress. Training a policy so the robot goes down safely when it loses balance, combining imitation learning with reinforcement learning. No results yet.
  • Panda arm pick-and-place pipeline: in progress. A simulation pipeline for a Franka Panda arm performing pick-and-place tasks, as a second track for manipulation policies independent of the humanoid's locomotion work. No results yet.
  • Humanoid Teleop : a wearable-IMU lower-body teleoperation pipeline built with a 6-person team as our UCSD CSE 145 final project, covering pose reconstruction, retargeting, and a PPO motion-tracking environment in IsaacLab with a handoff to MuJoCo. Simulation only.

Building in Parallel

The hardware team is building the physical robot at the same time the ML team is writing the policies for it. That shows up in nearly every team meeting, from mismatched mass and inertia estimates to actuator limits the simulator does not model yet. Everything I have worked on so far runs in simulation.
