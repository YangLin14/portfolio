Unitree G1 Ping-Pong Forehand

A Unitree G1 humanoid that returns a cross-court forehand into one of four target zones, built with Triton Droids on top of HOPE, the open humanoid table-tennis platform. A ball planner decides where, when, and how fast the racket must be, and a reinforcement-learned whole-body policy gets it there while imitating a reference swing. Simulation only for now; the robot has not arrived.

The task

The feed comes diagonally from the opponent's right. The robot has to strike the ball at the apex after it bounces (incoming vertical speed under 0.3 m/s), clear the net, and land the return in one of four zones on the opponent's right: open court, or one of three zones lined up along the end line. One policy covers all four zones; the planner picks the zone and solves the racket velocity that sends the ball there.

What I built

1. Apex, cross-court question bank

  • 🏓 Replaced the inherited Agibot A3 training questions, which struck falling balls across the full table, with G1-specific apex strikes aimed at the four zones
  • 📏 A success metric that follows the club's rally rule; HOPE's analytic success rate never clears the net

2. Training at rally pace

  • ⏱️ Late swing entry, timing jitter on the virtual strike, and a faster-feed bank so the policy trains at real rally cadence
  • 🤜 A fist-grip robot model with the racket across the closed hand, since the stock G1 hand cannot hold a handle

3. MuJoCo evaluation

  • 🔬 Per-zone sim-to-sim evaluator with a real ball plant, rally-pace mode, shove tests, and a camera model for a perception study
  • 📊 Picks checkpoints by this test; neighbouring checkpoints can differ a lot

4. Timing and aim fixes

  • 🕒 Traced early strikes to the deploy clock being one 20 ms tick short and added a deadline-based clock
  • 🎯 An aim offset in the planner for returns that landed 30–60 cm short, which tripled in-zone landings (12% → 35%)

5. Cluster training

  • 🖥️ Container-free IsaacLab jobs on the UCSD SDSC cluster, with resume that keeps curriculum ramps going and guards against known-broken GPUs
  • 🎥 A video → G1 reference clip pipeline (GVHMR + GMR), as in HITTER

Results

Best policy so far (fist grip, MuJoCo, deploy defaults, 240 held-out balls):

Metric No aim offset With aim offset
Ball hit 100% –
Struck at the apex ~95% –
Legal return ~86% 80%
Lands in the target zone ~26% 42%

Balance: no falls without shoves or with shoves up to 0.5 m/s, and 5% at 1.0 m/s (down from 26% for the earlier grip).

Findings

  • Spin matters more than cameras. In simulation, perfect ball tracking without spin drops legal returns from 61% to 10%. With spin known, mocap-level and iPhone-level tracking score 56% and 54%. So the plan is to estimate spin, not buy better cameras yet.
  • Fix aim in the planner, not the racket command. Re-commanding the racket for its velocity bias made results worse, because the policy does not follow out-of-distribution commands.

Status and next steps

  • Remaining misses are mostly scatter (~20 cm) rather than bias.
  • A balance-first run (lower stance, centre of mass over the feet, bigger shoves) is ready to train.
  • Before hardware: a 3D-printed fist holder, measuring the real racket, refitting ball physics on our table, and a G1 deploy runner.
