LLM Spatial Grounding (Talk-to-Robot)

A CSE 190 study of where LLMs fail when turning spatial language into robot goals. Gemini 2.5 Flash converts instructions into 3D goals, and a SAC+HER policy on FetchPush-v4 executes them. I was first author and tech lead on a 5-person team.

Setup

The pipeline is deliberately decoupled. An instruction in natural language goes to Gemini 2.5 Flash once, which returns a 3D goal. That goal is handed to a SAC+HER policy trained on FetchPush-v4, which pushes the cube toward it. Both the grounder and the policy read the same workspace definition, so a failure can be traced to one stage or the other. A regex grounder runs as a baseline to show which tiers need a language model at all.

The workspace is the FetchPush goal-sampling region, x in [1.19, 1.49] and y in [0.60, 0.90], with the robot-frame axis convention written into the prompt so the model does not have to infer it.

Benchmark

Five tiers, 20 instructions each, 100 in total. The robot and the policy are fixed; only the abstraction level of the instruction changes.

T0: literal coordinates

  • "push to (1.30, 0.85)"
  • No context needed

T1: named regions

  • "push to the upper-left corner"
  • Resolved against a region table

T2: metric offsets

  • "push it 5 cm to the right"
  • Needs the cube's current position as context

T3: object-relative

  • "push it next to the marker"
  • Needs reference-object positions as context

T4: abstract intent

  • "get it out of the way"
  • No spatial terms; acceptance regions were annotated by hand and frozen before the runs

Findings

Grounding success rate by tier and prompt variant, 20 instructions per tier.

Tier Zero-shot Few-shot CoT Regex baseline
T0 100% 100% 100% 100%
T1 100% 100% 100% 100%
T2 40% 45% 65% 100%
T3 0% 0% 5% n/a
T4 0% 0% 0% n/a

T3 and T4 use the uniform 5 cm acceptance radius described below. The regex grounder returns no answer on T3 and T4 by design, which is the point of the baseline: everything through T2 is parseable without a language model, and the tiers that need one are the tiers the language model gets wrong.

Each tier fails in its own way:

  • T2 hallucinates goals outside the workspace. In the few-shot run, 8 of 20 predictions were out of bounds. CoT prompting fixes the out-of-bounds problem and then places goals in spots the policy struggles to reach, so end-to-end success does not improve as much as the grounding number suggests.
  • T3 has a systematic offset of about 6 cm on the y-axis. It is not noise: the distance is 0.0600 m on nearly every one of the 20 instructions.
  • T4 under-specifies the intent. Mean error is about 19 cm. The model treats "out of the way" as "pick a corner," so success is close to a coin flip on which corner it picks.

The audit

An early version of the results showed T3 at 100% after T2 dipped to 45%, which did not fit anything else we saw. The cause was the acceptance radius: T3 was being scored at 8 cm while the other tiers used 5 cm, and the T3 predictions sit at about 6 cm, right in between. Re-scoring every saved run at a uniform 5 cm radius flipped 20 of 20 T3 instructions from pass to fail, and the rebound disappeared.

The reclassification is kept in the repo as results/uniform_radius_reclassification.md so the original and corrected numbers sit side by side.

Policy

The SAC+HER policy on FetchPush-v4 reaches 95% success at about 600k timesteps and about 100% at 700k, then settles back to around 94% by 890k. On T0 and T1, where grounding is exact, end-to-end success tracks the policy at 93% to 98%.
