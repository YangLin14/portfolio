LLM Spatial Grounding (Talk-to-Robot)

A CSE 190 study of where LLMs fail when turning spatial language into robot goals. Gemini 2.5 Flash converts instructions into 3D goals, and a SAC+HER policy on FetchPush-v4 executes them. I was first author and tech lead on a 4-person team.

Setup

The pipeline has two stages. An instruction in natural language goes to Gemini 2.5 Flash, which returns a 3D goal position. That goal is handed to a SAC+HER policy trained on FetchPush-v4, which pushes the block toward it.

View the repo on GitHub → (it includes a 7-task onboarding curriculum)

Course project paper, not peer reviewed.

Benchmark

We wrote a five-tier benchmark that holds the robot and the policy fixed and varies only how abstract the instruction is.

  • T0: literal coordinates, given directly in the instruction
  • T4: abstract intent

Findings

Accuracy drops roughly monotonically from T0 to T4, and each tier fails in its own way.

  • T2: hallucinates goals outside the robot's workspace
  • T3: a systematic magnitude offset of about 6 cm
  • T4: under-specifies the intent, so the goal is only loosely related to what was asked

The audit

An early version of the results showed accuracy climbing back up at T3, which did not fit anything else we saw. I audited the evaluation code and found the cause: the success threshold was not consistent across tiers, 8 cm in one place and 5 cm in another. The rebound was an artifact of the looser threshold. With one threshold everywhere, the trend is monotonic.

Policy

The SAC+HER policy on FetchPush-v4 reaches about 95% success at 600k steps and about 100% at 700k.

My Role

I was first author and tech lead on a 4-person team with Joseph, Nasser, and Waleed. I led the topic pivot after our professor's feedback, wrote the introduction and conclusion, and ran the audit that caught the false T3 rebound.
