Humanoid Robot Teleoperation

A low-cost (~$180 in hardware) wearable IMU pipeline that streams lower-body motion at 100 Hz with sub-50ms latency, retargets it onto a custom humanoid, and feeds a PPO motion-tracking environment in IsaacLab, with a handoff to MuJoCo for higher-fidelity checks.

Project Summary

This is the Triton Droids "Humanoid Teleop" project, built with a 6-person team as the final project for UCSD CSE 145. It develops a low-cost lower-body humanoid teleoperation pipeline using wearable IMUs, human pose reconstruction, retargeting, and simulation-based motion tracking, without relying on expensive motion capture hardware. Five BNO085 IMUs paired with ESP32-S3 microcontrollers stream fused orientation quaternions over Wi-Fi/UDP at 100 Hz, achieving end-to-end latency under 50 ms from wearable sensing to simulated humanoid output. The pipeline reconstructs a lower-body human skeleton, retargets it onto a custom humanoid robot model (ch_robot), and trains a PPO-based motion-tracking policy with residual PD control in IsaacLab, with an IsaacLab-to-MuJoCo handoff for validation, all for approximately $180 in hardware cost.

System Design

1. Wearable IMUs

  • 🩻 5× BNO085 IMUs + ESP32-S3 boards, strapped to pelvis, thighs, and shanks
  • 📶 Fused orientation quaternions streamed over Wi-Fi/UDP at 100 Hz
  • ⚡ Sub-50ms end-to-end latency, ~$180 total hardware cost

2. Pose Estimation

  • 🧮 Neutral-pose calibration and SLERP smoothing of IMU streams
  • 🦴 Forward-kinematics reconstruction of a 9-keypoint lower-body skeleton from 5 physical IMUs
  • 📷 MediaPipe + FastAPI RGBD camera baseline for comparison/fallback

3. Retargeting

  • 🔧 Adapted the Holosoma pipeline to a custom 10-DOF humanoid model (ch_robot)
  • 🪢 Maps the human skeleton into robot generalized coordinates (URDF-aligned)

4. RL Training

  • 🏋️ PPO motion-tracking environment in IsaacLab, driven by retargeted reference motion
  • 🎛️ Residual PD control: retargeted motion sets nominal joint targets, the policy learns bounded corrections

5. Sim2Sim Validation

  • 🧪 IsaacLab-to-MuJoCo handoff for higher-fidelity checks
  • 🛑 Real robot deployment intentionally deferred until this validation step is solid

My Role

I was on the ML side of a 6-person team. I built the live retargeting stage that turns streamed IMU orientations into joint targets for our 10-DOF humanoid, extended Holosoma's converter so retargeted motion could feed IsaacLab training (custom joint order, DOF-generic slicing, FPS parsing, headless mode) and validated it on OMOMO motion data, wrote the reward and policy design for the motion-tracking environment, and built the IsaacLab-to-MuJoCo handoff. Team lead Darin Djapri ran the training; Cindy Chen adapted the offline Holosoma pipeline and the RealSense baseline; Neal Jian, Tauhid Malik, and Parth Trivedi built the wearable hardware, firmware, and streaming.

Milestones & Future Work

Most of the core simulation-based teleoperation pipeline shipped: wearable IMU sensing, multi-node wireless streaming, lower-body pose reconstruction, sparse keypoint generation, pose clip export, human-to-robot retargeting, and simulation-based motion tracking. Real robot deployment and full-body teleoperation were intentionally deferred as future work. They require better root-translation estimation, contact-aware retargeting, and additional safety validation before commanding physical hardware.
